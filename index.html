MEHRSHAD ESKANDARPOUR
01 /BIO

I'm Mehrshad Eskandarpour, a Wireless Communications Engineer who somehow turned a fascination with invisible electromagnetic waves into an entire personality, a research career, and a concerning inability to leave technical problems alone. My journey began in Control Engineering, but after just one semester of getting lost in block diagram feedback loops and watching my poles migrate dangerously into the right half-plane, I realized that chasing signals through the air sounded far more entertaining. So I switched to Telecommunications at Iran University of Science and Technology (IUST), scoring 22.5 out of 20 in Signals and Systems and 21.6 out of 20 in Electromagnetics probably had something to do with it, too. Four years later, I had authored and co-authored 11 research papers, earned the unofficial title of "the student with the most papers," and completed 27 teaching assistantships across 8 courses, collaborating with more than 10 professors and serving as Head TA for most of them. From wireless networks and 5G to reinforcement learning, cryptography, and AI, I collected research interests like Pokémon, although Pokémon would probably have been cheaper and significantly better for my sleep schedule. And because being a student, researcher, and teaching assistant simultaneously wasn't quite enough, I decided to get my hands dirty in the real world. I started with an internship at the Telecommunication Company of Iran (TCI), where I learned the fundamentals of network cores and switching before joining Irancell Labs. At MTN Irancell, I worked on 3G, 4G, and 5G network configurations across four major regions of Iran, keeping networks operational and investigating alarms that seemed personally determined to ruin my peace. I'm currently working at MCI (Mobile Communication of Iran) known as Hamrah Aval, focusing on telecommunications transmission network optimization, where I finally developed a practical understanding of strictly increasing functions: my responsibilities keep growing, while my free time approaches zero. Somewhere along the way, I also became an AI and Programming Instructor at Irancell Academy, delivering 260 hours of instruction across four classes in just three months. After years of explaining engineering concepts at IUST, teaching AI felt like a natural progression. Apparently, I'd spent so much time trying to understand complicated things that I had become professionally qualified to explain them to others. Then came another plot twist. I applied for master's programs for the 2026 intake, received an admission offer from Finland, Belguim, Italy, and decided to turn it down. Not because I suddenly lost interest in studying abroad, but because I couldn't imagine making that move without my better half. So, 2027 it is. There's a particular reason for that decision, but we'll get to it. Meanwhile, in August 2026, I joined FarmadCo as a Project Manager, taking responsibility for solar-powered traffic signal infrastructure, technical teams, maintenance operations, and wireless sensor projects for data centers. My research interests have also continued expanding into wireless sensor networks, radio access networks, and intelligent systems. At this point, my professional interests are growing considerably faster than my available free time, which is already approaching a mathematically concerning limit. The publications, teaching experience, hands-on engineering work, and responsibility of managing technical projects didn't happen by accident. They took years of persistence, sacrificed weekends, unreasonable ambition, and a slightly unhealthy refusal to give up when things got difficult. And throughout all the academic chaos, questionable career decisions, and occasional emotional system crashes, one person has somehow remained remarkably consistent. Who has managed to stay by my side without submitting a single resignation letter. Surviving a relationship with someone who treats life like an engineering problem probably deserves its own Q1 publication. I'm even happier that I've shared the journey with someone who has witnessed both my greatest achievements and my most spectacular system failures. I couldn't find an appropriate section in my CV to mention my partner, so I decided to make room here. And no, I'm not accepting any more responsibilities. Unless they're exceptionally interesting.
